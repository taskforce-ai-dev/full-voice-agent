About SLIC.

Sri Lanka Insurance Corporation, commonly known as SLIC, is one of the oldest and largest state owned insurance companies in Sri Lanka.
SLIC has been serving Sri Lankan customers for many decades and is widely recognised as a long established, full service insurer.
The SLIC product portfolio covers motor insurance, life insurance, health insurance, property and home insurance, and marine and cargo insurance.
SLIC operates a nationwide branch network, backed by a round the clock claim support line.
This means customers anywhere on the island can usually reach an assessor or a customer service representative when they need help, at any time of day or night.


Motor policy types at SLIC.

SLIC motor insurance is offered in three main tiers.
These three tiers are comprehensive cover, third party only cover, and act only cover.
Each tier offers a different level of protection, and the right choice depends on the age of the vehicle, its market value, and how the owner uses it.

Comprehensive cover is the highest level of motor insurance protection.
Comprehensive cover includes own damage to the insured vehicle, third party liability for injury or damage caused to other people and their property, theft of the vehicle, damage by fire, and damage by natural perils such as floods, storms, and fallen trees.
Comprehensive cover is recommended for newer and higher value vehicles.
Comprehensive cover usually also supports optional add ons such as windscreen cover, riot and strike cover, and driver personal accident cover, which can be added for a small additional premium.

Third party only cover protects the policyholder against legal liability to other people.
If the insured driver causes an accident that injures another person or damages another vehicle or property, third party only cover will respond to that claim.
However, third party only cover does not pay for any damage to the insured vehicle itself.
Third party only cover also does not cover theft of the insured vehicle or damage by fire.
Third party only cover is often chosen for older vehicles where comprehensive cover would be expensive relative to the value of the vehicle.

Act only cover is the minimum insurance required under the Motor Traffic Act of Sri Lanka.
Act only cover provides the bare legal minimum of third party bodily injury liability, so that the vehicle can be legally driven on public roads.
Act only cover does not cover damage to the insured vehicle, does not cover theft, and does not cover fire.
Act only cover also offers only very limited third party property damage protection.
Act only cover is the cheapest option but gives very little real protection, and is generally only used when cost is the most important factor.


What to do immediately after an accident.

Staying calm and following a clear sequence of steps makes the claim process faster, safer, and easier for everyone involved.
The numbered steps below are the recommended actions right after a motor accident in Sri Lanka.

1. Ensure personal safety first. If it is safe to do so, move the vehicle to the side of the road so that it does not block traffic. Turn on the hazard lights so other drivers can clearly see the vehicle.

2. Check for injuries. Check yourself, your passengers, and anyone in the other vehicle. If anyone is injured, call emergency services immediately and request an ambulance. Do not attempt to move seriously injured people unless there is an immediate danger, such as fire.

3. Take clear photos of the scene. Photograph the damage to all vehicles involved, the position of the vehicles on the road, any skid marks, road signs, and the surrounding area. Photos are very useful for the assessor and for the claim file.

4. Exchange details with the other party. Write down the other driver's full name, phone number, address, driver's license number, vehicle registration number, and insurance details. If there are witnesses, ask for their names and phone numbers as well.

5. File a police entry when required. A police entry is required if there are any injuries, if there is significant property damage, if a third party is involved, or if the other driver refuses to cooperate. Go to the nearest police station and file the entry as soon as possible. Keep the police entry reference number safe, because it will be needed for the claim.

6. Call the SLIC claim line. Report the accident and request an assessor. Give the operator your vehicle registration number, your policy number if you have it, and the exact location of the vehicle. The operator will open a claim file and dispatch an assessor to the scene.

7. Do not agree to private cash settlements on the spot, especially if injuries or significant damage are involved. Let the insurance process handle it, so that both parties are properly protected.


The SLIC claim line.

The SLIC claim line is the central hotline for reporting accidents, theft, fire, and other motor incidents.
When a caller reaches the claim line, the agent records the caller's details, confirms the policy, opens a claim reference number, and dispatches an assessor from the nearest branch.
The claim line operates around the clock, including nights, weekends, and public holidays.
For the most up to date hotline number, callers should check their insurance certificate or contact their branch.

Typical assessor arrival times depend on the location and the time of day.
In urban areas such as Colombo, Kandy, Galle, and Jaffna, assessors usually arrive within about 20 to 30 minutes during daytime hours.
In more rural areas, or during heavy traffic and bad weather, arrival may take between 30 and 45 minutes.
If the accident is in a very remote location, the claim line will give a realistic estimate when the call is made.
The claim reference number generated during this call should be saved by the caller for all follow up conversations.


Assessor on site duties.

When the SLIC assessor arrives at the scene, or at the location where the vehicle is parked, the assessor performs several important tasks.
The assessor inspects the vehicle thoroughly and documents all visible damage.
The assessor takes photographs from several angles, including close up shots of specific damaged parts, and wider shots of the vehicle and its surroundings.
The assessor takes a short written statement from the policyholder describing how the accident happened, the time it occurred, and the exact location.

The assessor then issues a preliminary report, which is used to open or update the claim file.
Based on the inspection, the assessor decides whether the vehicle is safe to drive, or whether it needs to be towed to a garage.
If the vehicle is not safe to drive, the assessor arranges a tow service to an approved garage.
Under comprehensive cover, the tow service cost is usually covered by the policy.
Under third party only or act only cover, tow service costs are generally not covered, and the policyholder may need to arrange and pay for towing separately.


Required documents for a claim.

To complete a motor claim, the policyholder needs to submit a set of supporting documents.
These documents help confirm the policy, the vehicle, the driver, and the nature of the damage.
The typical documents required for a SLIC motor claim include the insurance certificate or a copy of the policy schedule.
A copy of the driver's license of the person who was driving at the time of the accident is also required.
The vehicle registration book, or a certified copy of the registration book, is required to confirm ownership of the vehicle.
The police entry or police report is required if one was filed for the incident.
Photographs of the damaged vehicle and the accident scene should also be submitted.
Repair estimates from an approved garage are required before repair work is authorised.
For injury or third party claims, additional documents such as medical reports, hospital bills, and third party contact details may also be required.
Submitting all documents quickly helps the claim move faster. If any document is missing, the claim assessor will tell the policyholder what is still outstanding.


Excess and deductible basics.

The excess, also called the deductible, is a fixed amount that the policyholder must pay out of pocket for each claim.
The insurer pays the remainder of the claim amount up to the policy limit.
Excess and deductible mean the same thing, and the two words are often used interchangeably.

The excess can be a flat rupee amount, or a percentage of the claim amount.
In Sri Lanka, the excess on a standard private car comprehensive policy is often a flat amount.
For a typical private car, this flat excess is, for example, somewhere around LKR 10,000 to LKR 25,000, but the exact figure depends on the specific policy.
For young drivers, newly licensed drivers, or for certain higher risk vehicle classes such as sports cars or commercial vehicles, the excess may be higher.
The excess is charged per claim, so each separate claim incurs its own excess amount.
If the claim amount is smaller than the excess, it does not make sense to claim, because the insurer would pay nothing, and the no claim bonus would still be affected.
For exact excess and deductible amounts on a specific policy, the caller should check their policy schedule or contact their branch.


Claim settlement timelines.

Settlement timelines depend on how complex the claim is, and how quickly all required documents are received.
Simple claims, such as minor own damage with clear fault and no injuries, are usually settled within about 5 to 10 working days after all documents are received.
More complex claims typically take between 2 and 8 weeks.
Complex claims include major damage claims, injury claims, claims involving disputes with a third party, and claims involving theft and police investigation.

Settlement can be done in two main ways.
The first way is cashless repair, where SLIC pays the approved garage directly after the repair is complete and the invoice is submitted.
The second way is reimbursement, where the policyholder pays the garage first and SLIC reimburses the policyholder after the claim is approved.
Cashless repair at an approved garage is usually the faster and easier option for the customer.


Third party claims.

A third party claim happens when the caller is not the SLIC policyholder, but was involved in an accident with a SLIC insured vehicle.
For example, if a SLIC insured driver hits another person's car, the other car owner is a third party claimant.
In that situation, the third party should still call the SLIC claim line.
The third party should quote the SLIC policyholder's vehicle registration number and, if available, the policyholder's name or policy number.

SLIC will then open a third party claim file, send an assessor to inspect the third party vehicle, and process the claim under the liability section of the SLIC policyholder's cover.
The third party should keep copies of all relevant documents, including the police entry, photos of the damage, and repair estimates.
Settlement of third party claims can take a little longer than simple own damage claims, because fault and liability need to be confirmed carefully.


Frequently asked questions.

Will my premium go up after I claim.
Making a claim can affect your no claim bonus, which is a discount given for claim free years.
Losing the no claim bonus usually means a higher premium at the next renewal.
For very small damage, it may sometimes be cheaper not to claim and to keep the no claim bonus intact.
Your branch can explain the exact impact on your renewal premium.

Can I choose my own garage.
Yes, you can usually choose your own garage, but cashless repair is only available at garages that are approved by SLIC.
If you repair at a non approved garage, you typically pay first and then claim reimbursement from SLIC.
Approved garages are listed on the SLIC website, and can also be confirmed with your branch.

What if the other driver was at fault.
If the other driver was at fault and is insured, their insurance should pay for the damage to your vehicle.
If you have comprehensive cover, SLIC can still handle your own damage claim and then recover the amount from the other insurer, in a process known as subrogation.
If you only have third party or act only cover, SLIC will not pay for your own damage.
In that case you will need to claim directly from the at fault driver or from their insurer.

Is theft covered.
Theft of the vehicle is covered under comprehensive cover.
Theft is not covered under third party only cover or act only cover.
If your car is stolen, you must file a police complaint immediately, and then call the SLIC claim line to report the theft.
You will need the police complaint reference, the vehicle registration book, and your insurance certificate to process the theft claim.

What if my car is a total loss.
A total loss is when the cost of repair is very high compared to the market value of the vehicle, or when the vehicle is beyond economic repair.
In a total loss situation, SLIC usually pays out the market value of the vehicle at the time of the accident, minus the excess and any salvage value.
The exact amount depends on the sum insured and the current market value of the vehicle.

How do I renew my policy.
Motor policies are typically renewed every 12 months.
SLIC normally sends a renewal notice before the expiry date, so that the policyholder has time to pay.
You can renew at any SLIC branch, through an authorised agent, or through online channels where they are available.
Do not let the policy lapse, because driving without valid insurance is an offense under the Motor Traffic Act.

Does my policy cover driving in India or abroad.
Standard SLIC motor policies cover driving within Sri Lanka only.
Cover does not automatically extend to India or to any other country.
If you plan to ship your vehicle abroad, or drive in another country, contact your branch well in advance to discuss the options.

What if I lose my claim reference number.
If you lose your claim reference number, you can call the SLIC claim line with your vehicle registration number and your name.
The agent can then look up your claim file and give you the reference number again.
Keeping the claim reference number saved in your phone makes follow up calls much easier.


Closing notes for callers.

If there is an ongoing incident right now, the fastest path is to report the accident through the claim line so an assessor can be dispatched to the scene immediately.
The assessor will come to the vehicle, inspect the damage, take photos, take a statement, and decide whether the vehicle can be driven or needs to be towed.
For general questions about cover, excess, required documents, and settlement, the claim line can also explain the details, or transfer the call to the right department.
Callers are encouraged to keep their policy number, vehicle registration number, and a charged phone handy when they call, so that the claim can be opened quickly and an assessor can be dispatched without delay.
