Activities and experiences at Riviera Resort make use of the Batticaloa lagoon, also called the Kallady lagoon, which is fifty six kilometres long. Sustainability at Riviera Resort. The resort was the first hotel in the Batticaloa district to build a biogas plant, commissioned in April 2015, which digests most of the kitchen organic waste and some garden waste and supplies around twenty percent of the kitchen's cooking energy. Garden waste is turned into charcoal and biochar, leaves are composted daily, and the slurry from the biogas plant is used as organic fertiliser across the gardens. Vegetables, fruit and herbs are grown on the property, and there are rabbits, quail and a duck pond that children enjoy. Many organisations use the resort as a model for good waste management practice.

Location of Riviera Resort. Riviera Resort is in Kallady, a real working village just across the lagoon from Batticaloa town, about a five minute ride from the city centre and the railway station. Kallady Beach on the Indian Ocean is about a ten minute walk from the resort, and the resort is next to the Old Kallady Bridge. Kallady is known for the Old Kallady Bridge and for the singing fish of the lagoon. Batticaloa, fondly called Batti, is on the east coast of Sri Lanka in the Eastern Province and is surrounded by lagoons and sandy beaches.

Things to see around Batticaloa from Riviera Resort. The Batticaloa Dutch Fort was built in 1628 by the Portuguese, taken by the Dutch ten years later, and today houses the District Secretariat; visitors can walk the ramparts for views of the lagoon and the old cannons. The Batticaloa lighthouse, twenty eight metres tall and built in 1913, marks where the lagoon meets the sea, next to the Lagoon Environmental Learning Centre and the Eco Park viewing tower. The Old Kallady Bridge, officially the Lady Manning Bridge, was built in 1924 and is the oldest and longest iron bridge in Sri Lanka, now a favourite spot for walking, cycling, angling and sunset views; a church shaped like a whale stands at its west end.

More things to see around Batticaloa from Riviera Resort. Kallady Beach is the locals' favourite beach, and the coastal cycle route runs from Kallady to Navalady beach where the lagoon meets the sea, with beautiful sunrises on the east coast. Gandhi Park is home to the Batticaloa Gate and the old British port crane, and Fort Park is a landscaped park popular with children in the evenings. The Batticaloa public market is busy every morning, especially on Sundays. The rare branched Palmyra trees near the Weber stadium and the Fort are a curiosity, and memorials along the coast remember the 2004 Boxing Day tsunami.
